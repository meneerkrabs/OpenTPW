# Staff placement, pick-up and drawing

Traced in tp.exe (Windows Sim Theme Park World v2.0); labels `[BIN:TPW-EXE:...]` in the code cite the routines. Code: `World/Staff/StaffPlacement.cs`
(tools and rules), `World/Staff/StaffSprites.cs` (sprite banks and clips), `World/Staff/StaffRenderer.cs` (drawing), `Hud/ParkHud.Staff.cs` (window and
buttons), `Hud/ParkHud.cs` (`UpdateStaffTool`).

## Hire and place

A hire (0x507bf0) creates no object: it builds the Place tool (CMMPlaceStaff) and flags the candidate as being placed. OpenTPW keeps the candidate in the
pool (`ParkStaff.PendingPlacementId`, exempt from expiry); the staff window closes and the advisor's help 0x135 (309, place your new staff member) is posted as the
original's hire handler does (0x49b9dd); the Place tool withdraws it when it ends (0x46c890). A click on a valid cell calls `ParkEconomy.Hire` and puts the member on the cell centre: state Idle, happiness and energy 100, patrol rectangle
the whole map; the role's constructor then runs its first search inside the click (`StaffWorldSimulation.Constructed`, tp.exe 0x4D46D0 entertainer, 0x4D63D0 guard, 0x4D7100 handyman, 0x4DA5B0 mechanic, 0x502C70 researcher), so the first job is chosen at the click and the sweep's first update goes on from it (a headless economy without a staff world still searches on its first update). Right click or Escape (`RmbCancel`) only clears the flag.

## Pointer timing

The hire and carry tools are their own mode classes (vtables `0x6FEA40` and `0x6FEA70`, not the tool object): they take the left **press** (slot +4: `0x46C8E0`, `0x46CC40`) and ignore the release, so a click on a valid cell completes at the press. The patrol tool (mode 0x36) is a tool object's and takes both corners on the **release** (`0x524960`, `0x525D..`): press, release, press, release. A right click cancels on its release when it was quick (200 ms, 8 px). See docs/UI.md.

## Pick up and drop

The per-employee window (opened from the staff window's employee view) has a Pick up button (the original's control 0x50e, `b_pick`), enabled by `CanBePickedUp` (0x505bd0): refused on strike (states 4,
5), in hand (7), repairing (13: `StaffJob.Repairing`) and escorting (18: `StaffMember.Escorting`), and when the member's own cell is not type 0, 1, 3, 9 or 10. The member becomes
`StaffState.PickedUp`; a mechanic's ride assignment and a handyman's litter and toilet claims are released at once. The Carry tool drops it on a valid cell
(0x509af0 → 0x505ea0): cell centre, `Navigation = null`, Idle, idle stamp 0, no jobs; the patrol rectangle is untouched. Cancelling drops it back on the cell
it came from; only one carry runs at a time (a new pick-up drops the previous member back); there is no guest pick-up.

Valid cell: inside the map, not unowned, type grass, path, queue or queue end, under no placed object or the prototype ride (GUEST-013: tp.exe `0x46cc40` also
refuses a cell whose track record is a walkable track kind, and refuses object footprints by cell type rather than by occupancy; the occupancy refusal stays for a world that keeps the cells it was given, `[EXT:staff-drop-occupancy-refusal]`, user-approved 2026-10-18, and GUEST-013 keeps only the track-record test). An invalid click
does nothing and the tool stays. The cursor is `Input.CursorTypes.Carry` (the original's cursor 9; `[EXT:software-pointers]`), an invalid hovered cell shows the blueprint's red
ground marker (UI-099).

## Saves

A member in hand is saved dropped on its old cell, as the original's save does (it first ends the carry tool, whose exit `0x46cdc0` drops the member, `0x516c80`); a pending hire is not saved. A member left in state PickedUp without a tool (an imported
original save) is put back to Idle by `StaffPlacement.Reconcile`.

## Drawing

The hire and carry tools draw the person in hand every frame at the mouse's world point, 5 height units up (the constant -5.0 at 0x6fe974 is subtracted from the point's height), with heading 4 (0x46c480 reads the point 0x7a1c90 through 0x45d610 and places the tool's sprite with 0x475480), also while the pointer is over the HUD; the sprite pass adds the camera's yaw sector to the heading (0x542010), so the person turns with the camera. The sprite, created by the tool's enter 0x46c730 with 0x475a10, is script 0x15 (0x74ed98: slot 6, frames 0-3 looping) of the role's sprite type (mechanics 6, handymen 5, entertainers 4, guards 7, researchers 8 in the table 0x763f88) with the candidate's variant: the person's own bank, kicking its legs. The art is in the `.TPC` the game draws ([GUESTS.md](GUESTS.md)); every staff `.FPC` holds slot 6 as 2 x 2 pixel placeholders, which is why the person in hand was invisible while OpenTPW drew the FPC. The world point is the picker's nearest hit, the terrain's or an object model's (as tp.exe's `0x45bf90` gives it; `World/Original/ObjectRayPick.cs`); the sprite keeps that full point, including the height of a model hit, and lifts it along the height axis by the tool's 5 units **and by the terrain's height under it** (`StaffPlacement.CursorEnginePoint`, `StaffRenderer.DangleAnchor`), while hover and drop cells still use the cell only. The terrain's height comes on top because the tool stores the picker's absolute height plus 5 (`0x46c480`) where the sprite pass (`0x542010`) adds the terrain's height under the sprite (`0x4527f0`, at `0x5420f3`) to the stored word of every sprite whose flag `0x1000000` is clear; the dangling script sets only `0x1200` (`0x74ed98`), and every other caller stores a height relative to the terrain (a guest `0x4f9e60`, litter 0). The tool's pointer is the `c_carry.ani` cursor drawn at its hotspot (0, 0), the top-left corner of its 32 x 32 frames, as the pointer strip is. The PC video (81:30) shows the person just below the carry glove, where this placement puts it above the pointed ground point (`GUEST-021`, the only part still open: a capture of the original's carry over ground of known height is needed). The hire window closes with the hire (0x49b9dd) and the pick-up button closes the employee window, so no window covers the park while a tool runs.

Staff are sprites (`esprites.wad`), not MD2 models: Generic\Handymen `SPR_HA`; Generic\Mechanics `SPR_FA`, `SPR_OM`; Generic\Guards `SPR_GU`;
Generic\Researchers `SPR_RE`; entertainers per theme (Jungle DI, EX, NA; Fantasy FL, GN, LA; Hallow DR, FK, HU; Space MO, SM, SP). tp.exe sorts a folder's banks by name (`0x541210`) and loads at most 1 bank of the handymen, mechanics, guards and researchers folders for a detail word (`GameOptions.NUMKIDS`) of 0 and 2 otherwise (`0x41AA40`; low.sam 0, med.sam and high.sam 2; the entertainers' folders load whole), so on the Low preset only `SPR_FA` of the mechanics is loaded. The candidate's draw `(R >> 2)` is reduced by the category's loaded count when it is made (`0x541f70`) and again by the loaded count of every load (`0x4f8b10` at `0x4f93aa`), so a park made on Low keeps only the first mechanic bank for good; `StaffMember.SpriteDraw` and `StaffCandidate.SpriteDraw` keep that stored draw, outside the world hash like the guests' kid-set draw (the hashed `Variant` stays the draw modulo the folder's banks). The hardware-class path of `0x41aa40` (the flags word `0x79d86c`, class `0x4000`) is only reachable through the launcher file `dialog.tcf` and filed under `[EXT:hardware-d3d-path]`; GUEST-020 keeps open when the original reads the limit (its sprite system's start `0x540900`). The same word limits the kid sets to 2, 4, 6 and 8 (`0x41A9D0`), which OpenTPW does not do yet. The bank is
the candidate's drawn variant (`0x541f70`), a member of an original save keeps the `mSpriteID` its person record saves, reduced by the loaded banks (`0x4f8b10`), and only a candidate or member of an OpenTPW park save from before the variant was kept uses `(NameIndex + Id)` modulo the folder's banks.
Clips are the programs of the table 0x75a418 (`StaffSpritePrograms`, `docs/STAFF-SPRITE-VM.md`): stand slot 0, walk slot 1 (the patrol walk shows each frame twice), run slot 2, work slot 4 (the sweep shows
each of 4 frames twice, repair is an 8-frame loop, research loops 4 frames and four steps of frame 0), strike slot 5 (4 frames and six steps of frame 0, looping), the dangling person slot 6 frames 0-3 (from the `.TPC`, as the `.FPC` holds 2 x 2 placeholders there), the guard's catch then carry slots 7 and 8. Resting members have no sprite.

**Sprite sources (GUEST-016 retired, UI-092's world sources).** Every placed member has the original's sprite wrapper (the person's words `+0xC` handle, `+0x10` pending template, `+0x14` pending interval; `StaffMember.Sprite`, `StaffSpriteState`) and, while it shows a sprite, a source: the bounded sprite VM (`StaffSpriteVm`) over the decoded programs. The events are the original's (`StaffSpriteEvents`; each is read in tp.exe):

- *State setters* queue a template (`0x4217f0`): `0x5054d0` state 0 stand 3 (`0x4fa460`), 1 and 4 the patrol walk 9, 2 the walk 1, 5 the strike 0x13 after clearing the person's angle (the heading sector 4); the handyman's `0x4d7330` 8 and 10 the patrol walk 9, 9 and 11 the sweep 0x11; the mechanic's `0x4da370` 12 the patrol walk 9, 13 the repair 0x12; the researcher's `0x502c20` 15 the research 10; the guard's `0x4d65d0` 16, 18 and 19 queue none. The entertainer's search (`0x4d46d0`) queues 13 when the bank lists one performance and else 13 + (its second draw mod (entries - 1)).
- *The walk step* (`0x4fa2a0`), when it is still walking: a guard with a speed bonus above 0 (the word read unsigned, 16 bits) runs (2), an escorting guard holds (0x17), anyone else walks (1); `0x475c50` compares the source's restart PC with that program's start and the template is queued unless it is equal (a person without a source compares false); then the interval, 62 over the speed truncated and clamped to 0 to 250 (`0x4d41d0`, 0 asks for none). A step that arrives or is stuck asks for nothing.
- *The guard's catch* (`0x4d6790`) queues 0x16 and the release (`0x4d6a50`) 0x18, explicit events: escorting alone does not request the catch.
- *The pre-update* (`0x505490` through `0x4fa870`, before the role's state handler, every update of every staff thing) applies the pending template (`0x475b80`: the current and restart PC, the stack and the depths) and then the interval (`0x475610`: no rebasing of the due time), clearing both even when the person has no source. A performance template (13 to 16, the bank's entries 0 to 3) takes the program, the slot and the slot's frame count from the bank; the entry's intro and hold bytes go to locals in `0x475b80` and are written only when a sprite is created with a performance entry, which no staff creation does, so the registers stay 0 and Hallow's `SPR_DR` (intro 2, hold 40) plays its eight frames like the others.
- *Lifecycle.* A source is made by the role constructors (`0x4d4140`, stand 3), the drop (`0x505ea0`, which then queues the stand) and the rest's end (`0x506d10`); the pick-up (state 7) and the rest (state 3) delete it at once. Creating or deleting a source leaves the pending words alone. A new source has no frame, the interval 62 and the first due time 62 ms after its creation (`0x4758f0`): it shows nothing before its first scheduler pass, and a person put down while the game is paused stays invisible until the clock runs.
- *The sprite scheduler* (`0x475360`) steps every source whose due time is before the pass's one clock sample, once, with no catching up, in the main loop's even 31 ms substeps (`0x54f5d7`, `0x54f5fb`), before the park turn of that substep: `Level.ScheduleSprites`, at `GuestSimulation.BodySpriteSampleAtTick` (`[EXT:fixed-60hz-clock]`, the sample the guests' bodies and balloons share). The tools' dangling person (`StaffPlacement.ToolSprite`) is a source of the same scheduler. The sprite pass draws a source whose output word is set at the slot and frame its registers hold.
- *Saves.* The wrapper and the source are saved with the staff world's actors in the resume file (an optional, validated field); a save without them, an original save (its sprite records are SAVE-013's, not applied) and a member whose state expects a sprite but has none (`StaffSpriteEvents.Reconstruct`) get a fresh stand source and the state's template at the first update. Nothing of it is hashed, and no pin moved.

The hire window's figure and the employee window's portrait stay on the host's renderer-call clock (UI-082, UI-092). A member's interval is 62 over its speed (doubled for the walk, 0x4FA2A0): 20 steps a cell walking, 10 running, from the step's own displacement (the staff world's `Walk`); the drawn point is the sub-turn point between the position at the start of the member's turn and his current one (`PersonInterpolation`, 0x4F9F00, 0x4FA030, 0x4FA870). `StaffRenderer` draws one quad per member in one draw call with the
guests' sprite shader and atlas loading and allocates nothing per frame once the members are known. A member's quad gets the original's depth pull of a pickable sprite, as a visitor's does ([GUESTS.md](GUESTS.md#rendering), 0x589B50); the person in hand does not, as the tool's sprite has no pick flag.

## Patrol areas (reviewed October 26, 2026)

A staff member patrols a rectangle of cells, not a route. Every hired member starts with the whole map (corner cell ids `+0x20A` = 1 and `+0x20C` = 0x4000, `0x506E90`; a cell id is y × 128 + x + 1).

**How a player sets one.** Open the employee's window (a click on the person, or a row of All Staff). Its footprint button (`b_route`, a toggle, help 86 "set a patrol area" / 87 "whole park") passes its new state to `0x4B5B50`. Switched on, it holds the member (`0x48C950`), builds the tool object `0x46C580(0x36)` (the generic tool class of the path tool, vtable `0x6FE9E0`), makes it current (`0x46C350`, which sets mouse mode 0x36 and clears the stored corner), closes the window and posts help 193; the mode change withdraws it. Switched off it only clears the member's area (`0x506E90`) and the window stays. The refresh `0x4B5AB0` checks the button when `0x506EB0` finds an area of its own (corner words other than 1 and 0x4000) and posts help 212.

**The two clicks** (`0x524960`, mode 0x36, on the button release `0x46C610`). The first stores the clicked cell (`0x81EDE4`/`0x81EDE8`) and the held member (`0x48C920`). The second orders the two cells (smaller x and y first, larger x and y second, both inclusive, so the same cell twice is a one-cell area), stores both cell ids on the held member (`0x506E50`), clears the stored corner and ends the tool (`0x52F200(0, 0)`). The only check is the preview's red verdict (below): a click while the ghost is red sets the refusal latch (`0x533931` → `0x527940(1)` → `0x816D48`), and the release handler tests it at `0x524A63`, plays cat_ui 0xAF (`0x524AA7`) and goes through the mode table (`0x52766C`, `0x527694`: mode 0x36 is index 8) to `0x5266A3` = `0x52F200(0, 0)`, so the tool ends and nothing is set. A cell that is not a path or not reachable is not checked. Escape ends the tool as it ends every mode object other than the default (`0x40C180`). A quick right click (released within 200 ms, moved at most 8 px) ends the mode too with `0x52F200(0, 1)` (`0x488434`) when `RMBCancelOn` (`[0x78D911]`) is set: the park view's mouse procedure `0x4881A0` reads it at `0x4881AE`.

**While it runs** (`0x5234D0`, mode 0x36). Before the first click the hovered cell is marked, after it the four edges of the ordered rectangle (LayLine `0x536100`, op 0x136: the top and bottom rows from x0 + 1 to x1 - 1 unless the rectangle is one column wide, the right and the left column). The markers are blue; they are red when every map cell of the rectangle is unowned land (flag 0x40) or a fence post (`0x532A50`, kept in `0x816D50`, answered by the validator `0x535670` for the op 0x36). The cursor is the default one (`0x52F950` sets none for this mode).

**The highlight.** The staff locator (Ctrl+S) draws the located member's rectangle with the same four lines in blue while the camera follows it (`0x4B3640`, message 0x1E of the locator panel `0x4B3790`, flag 0x1000, staff types 4 to 8 with an own area). The employee window draws nothing.

**What the area does.** Only the wander `0x4F9490`, used by the handyman, mechanic, entertainer, guard and researcher: a member outside the rectangle (`0x506ED0`, inclusive on both axes) first walks to a random reachable path cell inside it (`0x506F30`: up to 30 tries of two draws, "Could not find or reach a destination" and thought 22 when none); inside it the four neighbour candidates of every step that leave it are dropped, and when none is left the same walk into the area runs. Where the area cannot be reached the wander ignores it. The handyman's litter and toilet searches (`0x4D7100`), the mechanic's ride search, the guard's chases and the entertainer's performances never ask for the area, so a member leaves it for a job and walks back afterwards.

**Save and load.** The two words are part of the staff record (`+0x20A`, `+0x20C`); OpenTPW keeps them as the rectangle's cells and reads original saves' corner ids (`OriginalEconomyImport.ImportPatrolArea`).

**Differences.** Not patrol-specific: every OpenTPW park tool acts on the left press where the original acts on the release, and the pointer pick returns no cell off the grid where the original clamps the hover cell to the grid (`0x45D560`), so no off-map cell is ever stored; both are HUD-wide and out of scope here. Patrol-specific: the tool says its instruction in a message line besides help 193 ([EXT:strings] `PatrolAreaHint`).
