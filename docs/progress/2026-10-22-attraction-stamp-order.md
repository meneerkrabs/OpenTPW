## An original save's attraction time stamp is read in the loader's field order (October 22, 2026)

The eight words `tv[t]` of an attraction record were read as year, month, day, hour, minute, second and two skipped words. In tp.exe the attraction serializer `0x4DB7D0` writes them from `0x5FC660` (`FileTimeToSystemTime`, IAT `0x6FD0BC`, called at `0x4DB8EE` on the park time at +0x18), which stores wYear, wMonth, wDay (SYSTEMTIME +6), wDayOfWeek (+4), wHour, wMinute, wSecond and wMilliseconds into its out-pointers, and the loop `0x4DB8FB`..`0x4DB953` writes them in that order. Its read branch reads the eight words back (`0x4DC849`..`0x4DC874`) and calls `0x5FC5C0` (`SystemTimeToFileTime`, IAT `0x6FD150`) at `0x4DC8A1` with tv[0], tv[1], tv[2], tv[4], tv[5], tv[6] and tv[7]: the fourth word is the day of the week, which the loader skips. OpenTPW took the day of the week as the hour, the hour as the minute and the minute as the second and dropped the seconds, and its range checks let that through, so an imported object was dated up to almost a day early or six hours late: Wonder Land's newest object (its ferry) read as 00:17:42 instead of 17:42:30, sixteen park turns early, and every park's gates and lights (Saturday 2000-01-01 00:00) as 06:00. That build time feeds the scrap refund's first 30 days (`0x4E2290`) and the advisor's construction stamp, which it derives for an original-save park from the newest standing object (ADVISOR-009).

Theme Park Inc's `Game.exe` (no-CD, SHA-256 `77ea0c41...`) has the same code in its serializer `0x5DC430`: the write branch calls `0x7D91E0` (`FileTimeToSystemTime`, IAT `0xC68EA0`) on +0x20 at `0x5DC56E`, and the read branch calls `0x7D9140` (`SystemTimeToFileTime`, IAT `0xC68FD4`) at `0x5DD68E` without tv[3]. `ThemeParkIncAttraction` had the same mapping and is fixed with it.

`SaveTimestamp` now carries all eight words in the serializer's order, and both readers share one `SaveTimestamp.Read`. The day of the week is kept as read, and the converted words must lie in year 0–9999, month 1–12, day 1–31, hour 0–23, minute and second 0–59 and milliseconds 0–999. The loader itself checks nothing: a field `SystemTimeToFileTime` refuses leaves the thing's time as it was and writes -1 to a status word the loader never reads; OpenTPW keeps refusing such a stamp as a damaged save. `SaveTimestamp.ToDateTime` is the time the loader rebuilds, and the economy import's build tick is the first tick at or after it. The Mac build agrees: its serializer writes the out-words of the imported `TbTimeStamp::GetTime` (eight int pointers, `0x100DAFD8`) in order, and its loader passes every word but the fourth to `TbTimeStamp::SetTime` (seven ints, `0x100DB7D0`).

The data agrees. In the eleven private corpus saves every attraction's fourth word is its date's day of the week, and each save's newest attraction time equals the park analyser's `mLastObjectBuilt` (+0x21C30, the fifth of the six stamps the analyser serializer `0x4C5D70` writes) to the 100 ns: Wonder Land Sunday 2000-02-20 17:42:30, Halloween World Saturday 2000-03-18 03:02:30, Lost Kingdom Wednesday 2000-02-23 12:22:30, Space Zone Monday 2000-02-21 23:55:00, and 2000-01-01 00:00 in the restart saves. Each of the 24 attractions in Theme Park Inc's prebuilt save has its `mTimeOfLastSurveyReset` equal to its time stamp read this way; the constructor gives both one clock value (`0x5DB9DD`, `0x5DB9E8`).

New asset-free tests pin the order in both readers, keep any day-of-the-week word and refuse an hour of 24, a minute or second of 60 and 1,000 milliseconds. The synthetic import now checks the Belly Bounce's build tick as turn 15 (2000-01-01 15:37:30). The Theme Park Inc test body's stamp moves to the real order (Tuesday 2000-01-04 09:30), and the old invalid case that put 24 in the day-of-the-week word now puts it in the hour. A with-data corpus test checks the day of the week, the newest attraction against `mLastObjectBuilt` and the economy import's newest build tick against the same turn, and a Theme Park Inc test checks the prebuilt's survey stamps. All seven of these tests fail with the old field order.

Still open: the advisor derives its construction stamp from the newest standing object instead of reading the save's `mLastObjectBuilt` (ADVISOR-009); that is now the same turn for every corpus save.
