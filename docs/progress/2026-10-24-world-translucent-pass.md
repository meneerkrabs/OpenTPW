## The river and the harbour's glass roof draw like the original's translucent pass (October 24, 2026)

Part of issue 829 ("multicoloured river", "harbour glass roof"). OpenTPW put every terrain mesh and every object part into batches in file order, with depth writes on, so a translucent mesh drawn before the geometry behind it hid that geometry: the Jungle river showed a flat blue sheet with the bed painted over it in patches, and the glass of the arrival and departure roofs hid the roof's supports and the plaza under it.

- **Original.** tp.exe gives each face a state word from its texture's own alpha type (0x581790 -> 0x590C40). The renderer's mask `[[0x879AB0]+0xC]` is 0x3000 (0x5667D0): the frame batcher 0x581DB0 draws faces whose state has bit 0x1000 or 0x2000 (opaque 0x1256, alpha-test-0xF0 0x2756) at once, and 0x565590 keeps the rest (0x556, 0x200552), sorting each record's triangles by the sum of their vertex depths, largest first (0x565490). 0x582AD0, called after the batcher by 0x582170 at the end of the scene pass 0x576A00, draws those records last, sorted by 0x5829E0 on the average vertex depth, largest first. The state decoder 0x567620 gives 0x556 an alpha blend with an alpha test at 0x10/255 and keeps depth writes on. Both halves of 0x582170 run on the Direct3D path (mode 1 of `DAT_008BD524`, 0x44DD50).
- **What decides translucency.** The header of the `.wct`: the loader 0x564C00 loads the alpha chunk only at 32 bits per pixel, 0x574D3A sets the type word 0x25 for that and 0x27 when header byte 1 is also non-zero, 0x5755F0 turns them into texture flags 0x100 and 0x200. `TextureFile.ReadAlphaType` reads it (opaque, cut-out, translucent); over the 312 archives of the data, 2,488 `.wct` members that carry an alpha plane are translucent and 236 are cut-outs. `wr_water3` and `gte_rof2` are translucent; their low-detail copies in `stexture` have no alpha plane and stay opaque.
- **Port.** `Model` classifies its texture slots from the header and splits the faces of translucent slots into companion models, one record per (part, texture), so every consumer of `test.shader` (terrain meshes, heightfield, paths, objects, coaster, track, tour, lobby) gets the pass without per-consumer code; `OriginalTerrain` gives a mesh with translucent textures a model of its own. Companions are submitted to `WorldPass`, which `Level.Render` flushes after the other entities (the lobby after its layers), farthest first by the mean clip depth z/w of the vertices; a companion sorts its triangles farthest first by the sum of the corner depths (`Model.OrderTriangles`, a dynamic index buffer uploaded only when the order changes). Pool spray, pool water, particles and weather draw after the sorted list (`Level.SplitLate`, PTCL-905). `test.shader` tests translucent faces at 0x10/255 (vertex bit 0x20000000), cut-out faces at 0xF0/255 (bit 0x10000000, state 0x2756) and the rest at 0.1; the precompiled browser shader is regenerated.
- **Second review.** The pool swimmers draw after the pool water again (`GuestRenderer.DrawsAfterWater`, the late group of `Level.SplitLate`, whose two lists are reused each frame); the busy-park frame cost is back at the main branch's (zoom 110: 1.76/1.79 ms against 1.93/1.72, zoom 300: 3.12/3.16 against 3.20/3.14; the pass uploads every re-sorted index set before its first draw, a shared model keeps one sorted set per rotation, equal uniform blocks are written once, and `--frame-stats` counts the pass's records); a slot whose texture frames differ in alpha type changes pass with its frame (`Material.AddSlotAlternatives`, `CurrentSlotType`); the state bits 0x800, 0x5156 and 0x20000 are shown to be unreachable from the data (`Md2StateFlagCorpusTests`) and the wrap bit is `TextureSlotAddressing`'s; interface cut-outs (`ipan`, `smallguage`) draw with the original's 0xF0/255 test (`UiImages.CutOut`) and the black key of UI-034 is retired, the ID narrowed to the unread consumer of slot flag 0x2 (state bit 0x800000).
- **Before and after.** Native captures of the Jungle river (`--capture-camera -18,0,30,0`) and the harbour roof (`-10,-76,40,-90`): the river bed shows through the water and the cyan halo around leaves is gone; the roof panes show the supports, the road markings and the plaza.
- **Verification.** The rendering, terrain, object, particle, texture, model, shader, weather, sign, level, MD2, gate, UI, lobby, pool, WCT and alpha test filters with the game data (3,393 tests: 3,296 passed, 97 skipped, none failed), the rest of the test project with the game and Theme Park Inc data (7,748 tests: 7,635 passed, 113 skipped, none failed; no determinism pin moved, the change draws only), `BrowserPinsExclusionTests`, the precompiled web shader test, the browser pins build (`dotnet build source/OpenTPW.Web -p:OpenTpwDeterminismPins=true`) and the five native smokes (front end, sandbox, Jungle level, park resume capture and restore).
- **Tests.** `WorldTranslucencyTests` (header classification, the 32-bit alpha-plane decode rule, state words and the 0x3000 mask, the face split, the triangle and entity sort order, the level's draw sequence) and `TextureAlphaTypeCorpusTests` (every texture of the data, the named river and roof textures).

Still open:
- The original's deferred particle and weather batches (0x556, 0x200552) join the same far-to-near list as the world; OpenTPW draws them after the whole sorted list (PTCL-905).
- The alpha-reference index (0x10/0xF0 needs index 2, which 0x567270 sets when the device probe passes) and the reverse sort 0x5828F0 (render-flag bits 0x4 and 0x8, no writer found, and an instance flag 0x20000000 of the draw call) depend on the device and the draw call, not on the data; neither is reproduced and neither has been observed on a device.
- The atlas-page grouping of the batcher has no counterpart here.
